2. Employee Email Accounts: Employees use email for daily communication and sharing company information. If an account gets hacked, important information may be exposed.

3. Company Website: The website helps the company connect with customers and run its business. It should be secure and available whenever customers need it.

4. Hardware and Shared Computers: Employees use computers to do their daily work. If these computers are not properly protected, hackers or malware could access company information.

5. Cloud Storage: The company stores invoices and other important files in the cloud. This data must be protected from unauthorized access and accidental deletion.

2. THREE POTENTIAL THREATS

1. Hackers: Hackers may try to enter the company's systems to steal data or damage important files.

2. Phishing Attacks: Attackers may send fake emails to employees and trick them into sharing passwords or clicking harmful links.

3. Brute-Force Attacks and Insider Threats: Attackers may try different passwords to access accounts. Employees may also accidentally share sensitive information or misuse their access.

3. THREE VULNERABILITIES

1.Weak Passwords: Employees may use simple passwords or reuse the same password for different accounts. This makes their accounts easier to hack.

2. Insecure System Settings: If systems are not configured properly or employees have more access than they need, unauthorized people may access important information.

3. Outdated Software: If the company does not install security updates on time, hackers may use known weaknesses to attack its systems.

4. ASSOCIATED RISKS

1. Data Loss: If hackers access company systems, they may delete or damage important files, which can affect daily business activities.

2. Customer Data Leakage: If an employee clicks a phishing link and shares their password, hackers may access customer information and leak it.

3. Financial Loss and Loss of Trust: A cyberattack may interrupt business operations and lead to recovery costs. Customers and business partners may also lose trust in the company if their information is exposed.

5. SECURITY RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Maintain the Firewall: The company should configure its firewall properly and block unnecessary network connections to reduce unauthorized access.

2. Use Strong Passwords and MFA: Employees should use strong, unique passwords and enable multi-factor authentication (MFA) to make their accounts more secure.

3. Use SIEM Monitoring: A SIEM tool can help the security team monitor system logs and identify suspicious activities.

4. Build a Security Team: The company should have a responsible security team to manage risks, check security controls, and follow proper security policies.

5. Train Employees and Update Systems: Employees should receive regular phishing-awareness training. The company should also install security updates on time and give employees access only to the information they need.
